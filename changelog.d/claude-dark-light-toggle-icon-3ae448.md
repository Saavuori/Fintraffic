### Changed
- **Meri's theme switch is a corner pill**: dark and light were a pair of labelled buttons partway down the settings rail, which is a long way to go for a control whose whole job is one flip — and the rail had to be open to reach it. On desktop it is now the same round icon Tie has carried in the top-right corner of the map, a sun or a moon for whichever way it would take you, one click from anywhere. The phone keeps the pair of buttons in its settings panel: the corners there belong to the map and the tab bar.
- **Meri's replay starts from the map**: "Replay recorded movement" was the last row in the settings rail, filed under layers it has nothing to do with — it does not draw a layer, it takes over the whole map. On desktop it now sits in its own glass section along the bottom edge, in the exact place the transport bar appears once it is running, so entering replay expands the section in place instead of moving the controls somewhere new. It steps aside while that bar is up. The phone's settings panel keeps the row.
- **The map credit moved to the corner opposite**: the attribution ⓘ shared the bottom-left with the version badge on desktop. It now sits in the bottom-right, beside the locate button, and still unfolds into the full OpenStreetMap and CARTO credit when clicked. Unchanged on phones.

- **The phone's ship bar unfolds instead of opening**: the bar on the map showed a dot, the name and the numbers inline; tapping it swapped that for a page whose own title row was a bigger, better version of the same thing — so the bar visibly went away and something else arrived in its place. Both now render one component: the bearing arrow, the name over the ship type, and the speed-over-course readout, at the same size, in the same column, on the same row of the screen, with the track-history and close buttons in the same two slots. Opening adds what is underneath and moves nothing, and there is no drop animation left to read as a second window.
- **The call sign is up with the name**: it was a row in the identity list, four characters at the bottom of the page — but it is how a ship is addressed, not reference trivia. It now shares the type line in the headline, in the mono face, and keeps its place when the column runs short (the ship type gives way first). On a 375px-wide phone that line has room for the call sign and little else; wider phones show both.
- **The ship page's lower half**: below the voyage line it was eight identical full-width rows — heading, draught, MMSI, IMO, call sign, size, position, fix age — behind a second "More details" fold, which meant expanding the bar and then expanding again. The fold is gone, and what it held is now two blocks: what the ship is *doing* as four tiles read across as well as down, then what the ship *is* — the numbers you copy out rather than watch — two to a row, in half the height. Speed and course stay in the headline above and aren't repeated.

### Added
- **Half-day and full-day fleet replay**: the transport offered 1, 3 and 6 hours, which stops short of the span most questions about a day's traffic actually cover — a morning peak, or where a ship has been since it appeared. 12h and 24h join them, up to the day the server already allows; a wide window is thinned rather than refused, and the bar says "history capped" when it was. The row wraps instead of overflowing where five buttons and the transport won't share a line.

### Removed
- **Follow vessel**: the crosshair kept the map centred on one ship, which meant the map moved on its own and any drag silently switched it back off. Track history answers the same question — where has it been, where is it heading — without taking the map away from you. The button, the chase cam it drove and the fly-to that started it are all gone.
- **Tie's legend**: the filter panel ended in a colour key that restated every visible layer — four rows for the measurement stations, three for parking, four for charging — plus a paragraph on what half a station dot means. The markers already carry their colour in the layer toggle beside their name, and every one of them opens its own live detail on a click, so the key was a second, staler copy of what the map was already saying.
